            How it works

            Start point: each start point gets the loop that encloses the most area within its length. Loops start and finish at the road junction nearest the pin; if a bigger loop is a short way off, the run goes out to it and back the same way (that stretch counts twice).

            Area and Place: the roundest loop (4π × area ÷ length², 1 for a circle) that surrounds at least the share of the area you ask for; of two equally round loops, the bigger. Roads up to 20% beyond the area are used, or further if that isn't enough.

            Limits are optional (blank: none). If they can't all be met, the longest distance comes first, then the most climb, then the shortest distance and the area limits, then the share of the area, then the least climb; the loop says which it misses. Climb limits use an estimate from terrain data; the elevation profile is more exact.

            Hills: each meter of climb counts as that many meters of extra length while searching, so a flatter loop wins when it's about as good.

            Roads: paths, trails, sidewalks and rail trails are always used. Fence hops never cross a road, railroad, river or shore, and never start or end on a driveway, bridge or tunnel. Check the orange stretches before you run.

            The search keeps going until it stops finding better loops; press Stop at any time to keep the best so far. Big areas are searched in stages, first on major roads.
