ROUNDS 2-7 (2026-10-18), IN SHORT
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Modes
- Start point: the largest-area loop no longer than each pin's max length.
  If a better loop is a short way off (or the pin is on a dead end), the run
  goes out to it and back; that stretch counts twice.
- Area (circle, ellipse, rotatable rectangle, freehand) and Place (a real
  boundary, searched by name with a Search button): the roundest loop
  (isoperimetric quotient) covering at least n% of the shape (slider,
  default 90%). Ties go to the bigger loop. Roads are loaded 20% beyond the
  shape (its convex hull and north-south bounding box), widened to 40% if
  the target can't be reached.
- Loops never run a street twice and never cross or touch themselves.

Loop panel and exports
- Distance, area, roundness, coverage, time from pace (min/mi, min/km, mph,
  km/h), elevation profile with climb (AWS terrain tiles, 10 m data in the
  US, a sample every 20 m). mi/km toggle.
- Drag the green flag to choose the start; Reverse. Save in the browser;
  KML and GPX export.
- Fence hops, railroad tracks and roads marked private are drawn in orange
  and named in a warning (private stretches under 50 m are left out).

Roads
- Every OpenStreetMap road and path, from OpenFreeMap vector tiles
  (precomputed, worldwide, from a CDN), with Overpass as the fallback.
  Junctions are rebuilt from the tile geometry (bridges and tunnels kept
  apart, road pieces whose ends are within 2.5 m joined). Tiles stay in
  memory for later searches, are retried if a download fails, and start
  downloading as soon as a pin, shape or place is set.
- "Roads to use" panel: avoid motorways (off by default); fence hops (10 m,
  never from driveways, bridges or tunnels, never across roads, tracks or
  water; on); railroad tracks (on); prefer smaller roads (on). Private
  roads are used like any other. Indoor corridors and platforms never.

Search
- No time limit and no settings: it runs until better loops stop turning
  up; the best loop so far is shown live and Stop keeps it.
- Engine: an exact angular sweep (dynamic program over junctions sorted by
  angle) from many centres spread evenly in every direction, then
  block-by-block polish, in parallel Web Workers. The same search gives the
  same loop every time.
- Climb counts against a loop: 1 m of climb as 2 m of extra length.
- Up to 80 sq mi, and countryside up to 2,500 tiles, every road is loaded
  at once. Bigger: a rough loop on major roads, then all roads in a 3%
  corridor along it, improved stretch by stretch, then again along the
  improved loop while that gains at least 0.5%.

Answers kept as they are: no figure-8s or self-touching loops (Q45); the
search stops by itself (Q50); area mode may trade a little roundness for
speed (Q51); road downloads are not stored between page loads (Q48); trunk
roads stay in "avoid motorways" (Q54); one-way streets are ignored (Q58);
very large searches keep downloading every road along the loop (Q59);
cloverleaf self-crossings on huge loops are fine (Q61); tiles download as
soon as a pin is dropped (Q62); the corridor stays at 3% (Q66).

ROUND 8 (2026-10-18): DONE FROM YOUR ROUND 7 ANSWERS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q65: the margin around an area is now 20% (then 40% if the target still
can't be reached). Area searches got much rounder, see the table.

Q66: the corridor stays at 3%. (Tested 1.5%: half the tiles, but Kansas
came out a little less round, 0.709 instead of 0.713 at 75%.)

Q67: after improving the rough loop, a second pass loads a new corridor
along the improved loop and improves it again, and so on (up to 3 passes)
while a pass gains at least 0.5%. A second pass is cheap: about 98% of its
tiles are already loaded, so it adds about 20 s for Kansas. Honest result:
on Kansas the second pass only gains about 0.001-0.002 roundness, and
then it stops by itself. The big Kansas gain this round came from the
20% margin (Q65), which gives the rough stage room for a better shape:
Kansas at 75% went from 0.645 to 0.713 (see Q73).
Speed: fence-hop detection now only looks around dead ends (same roads,
faster), and the improvement stage reuses its work between rounds.

Q68: new checkbox "Prefer smaller roads" (on by default). While searching,
motorways, trunk roads and railroad tracks count as 8% longer and primary
roads as 3%. So a road right beside a highway is taken when it's about as
good, but a highway that is clearly better is still used (nothing is
blocked). The length, area and roundness shown are the real ones.

Q69: private stretches shorter than 50 m are no longer flagged.

Q70: before searching, every road gets its climb from the terrain tiles
(up to 64 tiles, zoom 13 or coarser; small searches take under a second
for this), and the search counts 1 m of climb as 2 m of extra length. So
of two loops that are about as good, the flatter one wins clearly, while a
loop that is much bigger or rounder still wins over a hill. In start point
mode the climb counts as lost area rather than used length, so a loop in
the hills still uses its whole length limit. Bridges and tunnels only count
the height difference between their ends. Examples (climb of the loop):
  San Francisco, 8 mi      4.1 sq mi, 690 ft  ->  4.0 sq mi, 245 ft
  Lawrence, 5 mi           1.5 sq mi, 315 ft  ->  1.5 sq mi, 270 ft
  Vermont hills, 12 mi     6.3 sq mi, 1,237 ft -> 6.5 sq mi, 1,130 ft
  Denver, 100 mi           590 sq mi, 3,057 ft -> 583 sq mi, 3,149 ft
                           (and no railroad tracks: 1.4 mi before)
Flat places (Chicago, Iowa, western Kansas) give the same loops as before.
