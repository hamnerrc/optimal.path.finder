ROUNDS 2-5 (2026-10-18), IN SHORT
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Modes
- Start point: the largest-area loop no longer than each pin's max length.
  If a better loop is a short way off (or the pin is on a dead end), the run
  goes out to it and back; that stretch counts twice.
- Area (circle, ellipse, rotatable rectangle, freehand) and Place (a real
  boundary, searched by name with a Search button): the roundest loop
  (isoperimetric quotient) covering at least n% of the shape (slider,
  default 90%). Ties go to the bigger loop. Roads are loaded 5% beyond the
  shape, widened to 20% and then 40% if the target can't be reached.
- Loops never run a street twice and never cross or touch themselves.

Loop panel and exports
- Distance, area, roundness, coverage, time from pace (min/mi, min/km, mph,
  km/h), elevation profile with climb. mi/km toggle.
- Drag the green flag to choose the start; Reverse. Save in the browser;
  KML and GPX export.

Roads
- Every OpenStreetMap road and path, from OpenFreeMap vector tiles
  (precomputed, worldwide, from a CDN), with Overpass as the fallback.
  Junctions are rebuilt from the tile geometry (bridges and tunnels kept
  apart). Tiles stay in memory for later searches.

Search
- No time limit and no settings: it runs until better loops stop turning
  up; the best loop so far is shown live and Stop keeps it.
- Engine: an exact angular sweep (dynamic program over junctions sorted by
  angle) from many centres, then block-by-block polish, in parallel Web
  Workers. The same search gives the same loop every time.
- Big searches (over ~80 sq mi): a rough loop on major roads first, then
  all roads along it.

Answers kept as they are: no figure-8s or self-touching loops (Q45); the
search stops by itself (Q50); area mode may trade a little roundness for
speed (Q51); road downloads are not stored between page loads (Q48).

ROUND 6 (2026-10-18): DONE FROM YOUR ROUND 5 ANSWERS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q49 / Q52 / Q53: new "Roads to use" section in the panel (all off by
default, remembered, applies to every mode):
- Avoid motorways, expressways and their ramps (motorway and trunk roads,
  their ramps, and any road tagged as an expressway).
- Allow fence hops: a straight link of up to 10 m from a dead end to a road
  it doesn't connect to. To stay safe, a hop only starts at a real dead end,
  never crosses another road, railroad, river, stream or shore, and never
  starts or ends on a motorway, ramp, railroad, steps, driveway (it would
  cross yards), bridge or tunnel. The loop's panel says how many it uses.
- Use railroad tracks (main lines, narrow gauge and heritage lines, not
  tunnels, yards, trams or subways). The loop's panel says how far it runs
  along tracks. See Q55 on why this is off by default.
- Paths, trails, footways, cycleways, bridleways, tracks, steps and rail
  trails were already all used and still are. Indoor corridors and station
  platforms are now left out.

Q53, Kansas at 75% stayed on major roads. Three causes, all fixed:
- The download for "all roads along the rough loop" covered the corridor's
  whole bounding box: 50,700 tiles for Kansas. Now only the tiles along the
  loop: about 2,000.
- Seams in the road data: one road is often split into several tile
  features whose ends lie a metre or two apart (at bridges, tile edges),
  and these weren't joined. That broke the main loop road in 5 places in
  Kansas. Ends within 2.5 m are now joined.
- The improvement step needed one unbroken sweep around a 1,100 mi loop; a
  single cloverleaf ramp anywhere made it fail. Now the loop is improved
  stretch by stretch (a few km to a few dozen km at a time); a stretch that
  can't be improved simply stays.
  Kansas at 75% now ends on all roads: 1,146 mi, roundness 0.62, coverage
  79%, in about 27 s from a cold start in the browser (was about 60 s, on
  major roads only).

Speed:
- Road tiles start downloading as soon as a pin, shape or place is set,
  before "Find loop" is pressed.
- 64 tile downloads at once instead of 16 (2-3x faster downloads).
- Road network build about 3x faster; faster noding and sorting.
- Solver workers start with the page and are reused between searches.
- The search settles after 40 quiet sweeps instead of 64: same loops in
  every benchmark, about 20% faster.
- Big searches (staged) are 2-3x faster and slightly better.
  Measured (same cached roads, this 2015 laptop, previous version vs now):
                               before                    now
    Lawrence city at 80%       roundness 0.429, 7.5 s    0.458, 2.6 s
    30 mi start loop, Chicago  57.3 sq mi, 17 s          57.5 sq mi, 7.8 s
    100 mi start loop, Denver  586 sq mi, 13 s           590 sq mi, 4.7 s
    Douglas County at 80%      roundness 0.49, 6.7 s     0.49, 2.7 s
  Browser, warm: a 5 mi start loop in about 1.5-2.5 s.

Fixes found along the way:
- At a bridge crossing (no junction), paths arriving along the two roads
  overwrote each other in the sweep, which could block the only way on.
- Results could differ slightly between runs because tiles arrive in random
  order; tiles are now assembled in a fixed order.
- With motorways avoided, a 100 mi Denver loop came out 107.7 mi (rough
  roads are simplified and shorter than the real ones); the improvement
  step now brings such loops back under the limit (100.0 mi).
