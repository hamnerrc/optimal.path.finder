ROUND 11 (2026-10-19): LIMITS, CLIMB IN COARSE STAGES, TIDIER PANEL
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Also fixed in between (round 10b): the iPad error "edge.coords" came from
the browser mixing a cached old solver.js with the new files. Every script
now loads with the release number (?v=12), so that can't happen again.

Limits (new section, every mode): min and max distance, area and climb.
Blank means no limit. Priority when they can't all be met:
  1. longest distance (how far you can go; per pin in start point mode)
  2. most climb
  3. shortest distance, smallest and largest area
  4. the share of the area to surround (Area/Place)
  5. least climb
  6. then the goal: most area, or roundest
A loop that misses one says which, in the warning; a loop held back from
its coverage target by a distance, climb or area limit gets a note instead
("Your climb limit keeps it to 42% of the area"), as that's expected. A min
above its max is flagged. Climb limits use the search's climb estimate
(terrain tiles); the elevation profile is more exact.
Examples: Vermont 12 mi start: 328 m of climb unlimited, 172 m with a 200 m
cap, 100 m with 100 m. 1 mi-radius circle in Lawrence at 90%: 157 m
unlimited; with a 60 m cap, a 7.3 mi loop covering 42% and climbing 60 m.
Searches without limits give exactly the same loops as before.

Climb in the coarse stages: country-sized searches count the climb again,
from coarse terrain tiles (an estimate), so the rough loop isn't set
somewhere hilly that the detailed stages can't get it out of. USA at 10%:
39 s (same as without), roundness 0.69.

Panel: shorter hints, the coverage target as one line ("Surround at least
[90] [% of the area]"), Limits and "Roads & hills" as collapsible sections,
and the long explanations moved to a "How it works" section at the bottom.

Note on timings: the tile server's speed varies through the day. Kansas at
75% took 42 s this morning and 60-64 s this evening, with the old and new
code alike.

ROUND 10 (2026-10-19): AREA LENGTH LIMIT, COVERAGE UNITS, HILLS SLIDER, SPEED
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Asked for: a max loop length in area mode that overrides the coverage
target; the coverage slider as a text box taking a percentage, sq mi or
sq km; a Hills slider from "No cost" to "Avoid hills avidly" (10x); USA at
10% in under a minute without straining the browser's memory; and other
sensible improvements.

Area and Place
- Coverage is a text box with a unit menu: "% of the area", "sq mi" or
  "sq km". Switching the unit converts the number, so the target stays the
  same. Under it: the same target in the other terms, and how long the
  loop must be at least (a perfect circle around that much). An area
  bigger than the whole place is capped at all of it, and says so.
- "Max loop length (optional)". The limit always wins: the loop stays
  within it, and if the target can't be reached that way, you get the loop
  within the limit that surrounds the most. That's shown as a note, not a
  warning ("Your 4 mi limit keeps it to 28% of the area ..."), and the hint
  warns before searching when the limit is too short for the target.
  E.g. Kansas at 75% with a 600 mi limit: 599 mi, 29% (a perfect circle of
  600 mi would cover 35%), 23 s.

Roads to use
- "Prefer flatter loops" is now a Hills slider: No cost (0) ... Avoid hills
  avidly (each meter of climb counts as 10 m of extra length), default 3
  as before. At "No cost" the climb isn't read at all.

Speed and memory (Chrome, this 2015 laptop, nothing downloaded beforehand)
  United States at 10%   failed before (an error in the first stage:
                         too many roads for one map); now about 50 s, roundness 0.69
                         to 0.72 depending on the run's rough loop, tab
                         peaking around 1.4-1.6 GB (under 1 GB of live
                         data). About 40 s if the place was picked 10 s
                         before pressing Find (tiles start downloading).
  Kansas at 75%          about 3 min -> 42-46 s, and rounder: 0.713 -> 0.73
  Kansas at 63%          0.750 -> 0.749, 49 s -> 12 s (cached roads)
  Everything else in the test set gives the same loops (Denver 100 mi:
  583 instead of 584 sq mi).
How:
- Tile servers answer about 150 requests a second, whatever the tile size,
  so the number of tiles is the cost. A corridor now takes at most about
  1,500 tiles: if full-detail (zoom 14) tiles would need more, zoom 13 or
  12 tiles (every road but paths/tracks, or also no service roads), which
  cover 4 or 16 times the area each. For Kansas that's 900 zoom 12 tiles
  instead of 17,700 zoom 14 ones, and since the corridor can then stay
  wide, the loop got rounder.
- Loops expected to be over 2,000 km (a share of a country): the rough
  loop uses motorways, trunk and primary roads from 390 zoom 7 tiles, one
  corridor pass, and no climb (country-wide terrain tiles only show hills
  kilometers wide).
- While a search settles, the tiles along its best loop start downloading
  in the background, so the next corridor is mostly there already.
- Fixed: road pieces cut at a tile edge in coarse tiles could end ~10 m
  apart and weren't joined, which broke corridors in two (the US loop
  failed at the Idaho border); and a corridor followed the loop simplified
  to 400 points, kilometers off a 5,000 mi loop.
- Memory: road geometry in flat arrays instead of an object per point,
  water layers skipped in coarse tiles, the road network only drawn when
  small enough (else just major roads, or none: the Road network section
  says so), two search workers instead of three for very big graphs, and
  each stage's data released when it's done.
- The rough stage of a staged search settles sooner (it only sets the
  shape), and very long loops refine stretches of at least 1/400 of the
  loop.

Also
- "Find loop" shows the time while searching (Searching... 0:42).
- A loop that misses the coverage target by a rounding hair (89.7% of 90%)
  no longer pops up "doesn't meet every requirement".

Known limits: on a 2015 laptop the US search is just under a minute; the
roundness of country-sized loops varies a few percent between runs with
different numbers of CPU cores (the rough loop differs).

ROUNDS 2-7 (2026-10-18), IN SHORT
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Modes
- Start point: the largest-area loop no longer than each pin's max length.
  If a better loop is a short way off (or the pin is on a dead end), the run
  goes out to it and back; that stretch counts twice.
- Area (circle, ellipse, rotatable rectangle, freehand) and Place (a real
  boundary, searched by name with a Search button): the roundest loop
  (isoperimetric quotient) covering at least n% of the shape (slider,
  default 90%). Ties go to the bigger loop. Roads are loaded 20% beyond the
  shape (its convex hull and north-south bounding box), widened to 40% if
  the target can't be reached.
- Loops never run a street twice and never cross or touch themselves.

Loop panel and exports
- Distance, area, roundness, coverage, time from pace (min/mi, min/km, mph,
  km/h), elevation profile with climb (AWS terrain tiles, 10 m data in the
  US, a sample every 20 m). mi/km toggle.
- Drag the green flag to choose the start; Reverse. Save in the browser;
  KML and GPX export.
- Fence hops, railroad tracks and roads marked private are drawn in orange
  and named in a warning (private stretches under 50 m are left out).

Roads
- Every OpenStreetMap road and path, from OpenFreeMap vector tiles
  (precomputed, worldwide, from a CDN), with Overpass as the fallback.
  Junctions are rebuilt from the tile geometry (bridges and tunnels kept
  apart, road pieces whose ends are within 2.5 m joined). Tiles stay in
  memory for later searches, are retried if a download fails, and start
  downloading as soon as a pin, shape or place is set.
- "Roads to use" panel: avoid motorways (off by default); fence hops (10 m,
  never from driveways, bridges or tunnels, never across roads, tracks or
  water; on); railroad tracks (on); prefer smaller roads (on); prefer
  flatter loops (on). Private roads are used like any other. Indoor
  corridors and platforms never.

Search
- No time limit and no settings: it runs until better loops stop turning
  up; the best loop so far is shown live and Stop keeps it.
- Engine: an exact angular sweep (dynamic program over junctions sorted by
  angle) from many centres spread evenly in every direction, then
  block-by-block polish, in parallel Web Workers. The same search gives the
  same loop every time.
- "Prefer flatter loops" (on): 1 m of climb counts as 3 m of extra length.
- Up to 80 sq mi, and countryside up to 2,500 tiles, every road is loaded
  at once. Bigger: a rough loop on major roads, then all roads in a 3%
  corridor along it, improved stretch by stretch, then again along the
  improved loop while that gains at least 0.5%.

Answers kept as they are: no figure-8s or self-touching loops (Q45); the
search stops by itself (Q50); area mode may trade a little roundness for
speed (Q51); road downloads are not stored between page loads (Q48); trunk
roads stay in "avoid motorways" (Q54); one-way streets are ignored (Q58);
very large searches keep downloading every road along the loop (Q59);
cloverleaf self-crossings on huge loops are fine (Q61); tiles download as
soon as a pin is dropped (Q62); the corridor stays at 3% (Q66).
