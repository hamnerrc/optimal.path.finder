ROUNDS 2-6 (2026-10-18), IN SHORT
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Modes
- Start point: the largest-area loop no longer than each pin's max length.
  If a better loop is a short way off (or the pin is on a dead end), the run
  goes out to it and back; that stretch counts twice.
- Area (circle, ellipse, rotatable rectangle, freehand) and Place (a real
  boundary, searched by name with a Search button): the roundest loop
  (isoperimetric quotient) covering at least n% of the shape (slider,
  default 90%). Ties go to the bigger loop. Roads are loaded 5% beyond the
  shape, widened to 20% and then 40% if the target can't be reached.
- Loops never run a street twice and never cross or touch themselves.

Loop panel and exports
- Distance, area, roundness, coverage, time from pace (min/mi, min/km, mph,
  km/h), elevation profile with climb. mi/km toggle.
- Drag the green flag to choose the start; Reverse. Save in the browser;
  KML and GPX export.

Roads
- Every OpenStreetMap road and path, from OpenFreeMap vector tiles
  (precomputed, worldwide, from a CDN), with Overpass as the fallback.
  Junctions are rebuilt from the tile geometry (bridges and tunnels kept
  apart, road pieces whose ends are within 2.5 m joined). Tiles stay in
  memory for later searches and start downloading as soon as a pin, shape
  or place is set.
- "Roads to use" panel: avoid motorways (motorway and trunk roads, their
  ramps, roads tagged as expressways), fence hops (10 m, never from
  driveways, bridges or tunnels, never across roads, tracks or water),
  railroad tracks. Indoor corridors and platforms are never used.

Search
- No time limit and no settings: it runs until better loops stop turning
  up; the best loop so far is shown live and Stop keeps it.
- Engine: an exact angular sweep (dynamic program over junctions sorted by
  angle) from many centres, then block-by-block polish, in parallel Web
  Workers. The same search gives the same loop every time.
- Big searches: a rough loop on major roads first, then all roads in a
  corridor along it, improved stretch by stretch.

Answers kept as they are: no figure-8s or self-touching loops (Q45); the
search stops by itself (Q50); area mode may trade a little roundness for
speed (Q51); road downloads are not stored between page loads (Q48); trunk
roads stay in "avoid motorways" (Q54); one-way streets are ignored (Q58);
very large searches keep downloading every road along the loop (Q59);
cloverleaf self-crossings on huge loops are fine (Q61); tiles download as
soon as a pin is dropped (Q62).

ROUND 7 (2026-10-18): DONE FROM YOUR ROUND 6 ANSWERS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q55 / Q56 / Q57: fence hops and railroad tracks are now on by default, and
roads and paths marked private or no entry (access=no/private, foot=no)
are used like any other. When a loop uses any of them, the loop panel
shows a warning ("It uses 2 fence hops... check they can be crossed",
"154 mi of it runs along railroad tracks...", "0.3 mi of it is on roads
or paths marked private...") and those stretches are drawn in orange on
the map (a hop also gets a dot), with a label on hover. Saved loops keep
the marks. Your earlier choices for these two boxes were reset once to the
new defaults.

Q60: the corridor for big searches is now 3% of the rough loop's size (its
length / pi) to either side, at least 1 km. Kansas: about 15 km, 14,000
tiles. The stretches improved one at a time now reach up to a twelfth of
the loop (85 km for Kansas) instead of 25 km. See Q66 for the time.

Q63: elevation now comes from AWS Terrain Tiles (free, served from a CDN,
no request limit): 10 m data in the US, 30 m or better elsewhere, read
from the tile images in the browser. Samples every 20 m (up to 1,000 per
loop) instead of every 40 m (up to 200). Open-Meteo is the fallback.

Q64: tested on 15 places (table below). That turned up several problems,
all fixed:
- Countryside searches were badly broken. Anything over 80 sq mi (a loop
  over 10 mi) went to the staged search, whose first stage only has major
  roads, of which farmland has few: a 20 mi loop near Boone, Iowa came out
  27 mi long; a 50 mi loop in western Kansas and a 12 mi loop in Vermont
  failed ("no roads here that form a loop"). Now an area loads every road
  at once whenever that's few enough tiles and road points (up to 2,500
  tiles holding 800,000 points); only dense big areas use stages.
- The block polish almost never ran. A footpath or drive that loops back
  across itself (common: turnarounds, park paths) broke the block map for
  every loop around it, so in most searches over a few miles the polish
  silently gave up. Fixed: such roads are cut at their own crossing in the
  block map. (Also: a fence hop could cross back over its own road.)
- The search centres were spread over far fewer directions than intended:
  the evenly spread sequence lined up with the every-third-sweep
  re-centring, so most directions around a start were never tried (a 50 mi
  farm loop found 145 sq mi instead of 150). Fixed, and start-point
  centres now sit about a loop's radius from the start, where they belong.
- With several workers, one that found no loop at all made the search wait
  forever (it never finished). Fixed.
- One failed tile out of thousands (a dropped connection) threw away the
  whole improvement stage of a big search. Tiles are now retried.
- The 5% margin around an area is now its convex hull and its north-south
  bounding box together, so a square of grid streets around a round area
  is possible (a 6 mi circle around Ames, Iowa: roundness 0.64 -> 0.75).
