  point that is no longer than its max length (roads are loaded within half

- Estimated time from a run pace or bike speed.

- Saved loops (this browser only), with their elevation data.

Done:
- Q40: pushed to main, so the site updates on GitHub Pages
  (https://hamnerrc.github.io/optimal.path.finder/).
- Q41: place search stays search-by-name.
- Q42 + "no time limit": new loop search.
    * The road graph is shrunk before searching. Dead-end streets, and
      whole trees of streets hanging off them, can never be part of a ring,
      so they are pruned. Chains of road between junctions are merged into
      single edges. That removes about 40% of the nodes in Lawrence.
    * Waypoints snap to junctions with a k-d tree (it used to be a grid
      scan that was very slow for points far from roads; that alone made
      big Place searches about 5x faster). Routing uses a slightly greedy
      A* that explores far fewer nodes.
    * One search per spare CPU core runs in parallel, each with its own
      random seed. Each keeps a small pool of good loops from different
      starting shapes and keeps improving them, with a hill climb over
      neighbouring junctions at the end of each round.
    * The "Search time" menu is gone. A search ends by itself once enough
      rounds in a row (12 + 4 per CPU worker, and at least half the rounds
      it took to find the best loop) haven't improved the loop by more than
      0.2%. The progress bar shows how settled the search is: it fills as
      rounds go by without a better loop and drops back when one turns up.
      Stop still keeps the best loop so far.
    Measured on saved Lawrence road data (3 searches in parallel, the old
    solver at its "Normal" 8 s):
                              old (8 s)        new
      5 mi start loop         1.27 sq mi       1.33-1.35 sq mi in 1-2 s
      10 mi start loop        5.85 sq mi       5.2-6.0 sq mi in 9-12 s
      rectangle at 90%        roundness 0.49   0.54 in 6-12 s
      Lawrence city at 80%    roundness 0.2-0.3 (20% margin, 30 s);
                              now 0.53 with a 20% margin, but see Q44
- Deleting a start point hides its roads, but the download is kept in
  memory. A new start point whose area lies inside an earlier download
  needs no new download. Up to 8 downloads (about 400k roads in total) are
  kept, and they're lost when the page is reloaded.
- Place search: the search now asks Nominatim for names only. The
  boundary is downloaded only for the place you pick, already simplified
  by the server to the detail its size needs. Lake Michigan went from
  200 KB to under 1 KB for the result list.
- Area/Place margin cut from 20% to 5% (CONFIG.areaMargin = 1.05).
- "Cross a road twice" (start point mode): the loop no longer has to pass
  through the start. If a better loop is a short way off (or the pin is on
  a dead-end street), the run goes out to it and back the same way. That
  stretch counts twice toward the max length, and the loop's panel says
  how long it is. The pin now snaps to the nearest road node of any kind,
  including the end of a dead-end street, not only to intersections.

Done:
- Q44 (b): area and place searches load roads 5% beyond the area. If the
  coverage target can't be reached with those, the search repeats with
  20% and then 40% by itself ("Roads near the area only reach 77% coverage.
  Widening the search to 20% around it…"). The hint under the coverage
  slider says so.
- Q45: unchanged.
- Q46: the Place tab has a Search button next to the box (Enter still works).
- Q48 / "precompute major roads of the world": roads now come from
  OpenFreeMap's vector tiles, which are exactly that: every OpenStreetMap
  road on Earth, precomputed, rebuilt weekly and served from a CDN, free and
  without keys. A search fetches just the tiles it needs, in parallel, and
  keeps them in memory, so nearby searches reuse them. Hosting our own copy
  wasn't an option: the world's roads are several GB and GitHub Pages
  allows 1 GB. Tiles don't say which roads meet at a junction, so the app
  rebuilds junctions from the geometry (roads that share a point, end on
  another road or cross it are joined, except bridges and tunnels). On
  Lawrence it matches the Overpass data to within about 3%. If the tile
  server is unreachable, it falls back to Overpass as before.
    Downloads: a 10 mi loop needs about 70 tiles, 2 MB, 1-4 s. Overpass
    took tens of seconds for the same area (10-70 MB).
- Q42/Q47 "blazing fast, best route, consistent": new search engine.
    * Angular sweep: for a centre point and a junction, the best loop
      through that junction that goes once around the centre (always moving
      forward in angle) is found exactly, by sorting the junctions by angle
      and running a dynamic program over (junction, distance so far). About
      25 ms for 20,000 junctions. The search sweeps many centres spread
      evenly around the start (or the area).
    * Block polish: a loop is the boundary of a set of city blocks. The best
      loops are improved by adding or removing single blocks and by
      rerouting stretches of the loop along nearby streets, hundreds of
      thousands of changes per second. This fixes what a sweep can't do (winding
      streets) and grows area loops to the coverage target. Loops still
      never cross or touch themselves; a bridge over another road is a
      crossing the loop may use only once, straight on.
    * The same search on the same computer gives the same loop every time
      (the sequence of centres is fixed and the stopping rule counts sweeps
      in order). Across computers with 1 to 6 parallel searches the results
      matched or were within about 2% in my tests.
    * Measured on saved Lawrence road data, 3 parallel searches, on this
      2015 laptop:
                              round 4                  now
      5 mi start loop         1.33-1.35 sq mi, 1-2 s   1.39 sq mi, 0.6 s
      10 mi start loop        5.2-6.0 sq mi, 9-12 s    6.06 sq mi, 2-3 s
      rectangle at 90%        roundness 0.54, 6-12 s   0.52-0.54, under 1 s
      Lawrence city at 80%    roundness 0.53, 43 s     0.49-0.50, 2-3 s
        (the last two with the 20% margin)
- Gigantic searches. Above about 80 sq mi (a 10 mi loop) the search finds
  a rough loop on major roads from coarser tiles, then loads all roads in a
  1 km corridor along it and searches again with them. The final loop
  always follows the real road shapes. In the browser, from a cold start:
      30 mi loop in central Chicago      about 10-15 s
      100 mi loop around Denver          about 15-26 s
      Douglas County, KS at 80%          about 20 s (incl. widening to 20%)
  Most of that time is downloading tiles the first time.
- The road data credit (OpenFreeMap, OpenMapTiles, OpenStreetMap
  contributors) is now in the map's attribution line, as their licenses
  require.
